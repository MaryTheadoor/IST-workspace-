## 2. The normalization question this memo exists to settle

"Vol(SU(3))" is not unique until the measure is fixed. Two candidates:

| Normalization | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Topological** (sphere-product) | Vol(S⁵)·Vol(S³) = π³·2π² = **2π⁵** | Phase 49; the fibration SU(3) → S⁵ with SU(2) = S³ fiber |
| **Haar** (bi-invariant, trace normalization) | **√3π⁵** = (√3/2)·2π⁵ | Boya–Sudarshan–Tilma 2002, eq. (5.5) ([arXiv:math-ph/0210033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210033)); Tilma–Sudarshan 2002 ([arXiv:math-ph/0205016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0205016)); Coquereaux 2021 |

The two differ by the metric-dependent factor √3/2 ≈ 0.866, which enters
through the Killing-form normalization of the bi-invariant metric (the BST
trace normalization), not through any topological content.

**The empirical selection is decisive:**

$$N_c\cdot\text{Vol}_{\text{topo}} = 6\pi^5 = 1836.12 \quad (0.0019\%\text{ low})$$
$$N_c\cdot\text{Vol}_{\text{Haar}} = 3\sqrt{3}\,\pi^5 = 1590.1 \quad (13.4\%\text{ low})$$

The data select the **topological** normalization at the level the ratio is
known (10⁻⁵). If the Haar measure were the physical one, the framework's
headline duality would be off by 13.4% — 7,000× worse. This is a
normalization *measurement*, not a convention choice: the sphere-product
volume is what the empirical proton/electron phase-space balance actually
pays.

## 3. Why the topological volume is the right one

The duality is a **degrees-of-freedom count**, and degrees of freedom are
counted by the topological phase-space volumes of the relevant spheres:

- **S³ = SU(2):** the unconfined lepton's spinorial phase space. Vol(S³) =
  2π² is the standard unit-sphere volume — the same object that counts
  SU(2) states.
- **S⁵ = SU(3)/SU(2):** the coset of color orientations of a confined
  quark relative to the SU(2) subgroup that leaves it fixed. Vol(S⁵) = π³.
- **N_c = 3:** the color multiplicity of the quarks inside the proton.

The Haar-measure factor √3/2 would instead weight the *metric geometry* of
the group (the Killing-form convention), which is not what a phase-space
balance pays. The framework's claim is therefore precisely:

> **The proton/electron mass ratio pays for the topological volume of
> SU(3), not its metric volume — and the data agree at 10⁻⁵.**
