## The Puzzle

In special relativity, $c$ is the speed of light, but what time interval is used in $c = \text{distance}/\text{time}$? The equation $E = mc^2$ does not contain an explicit time; it simply converts mass (kg) to energy (J) via a fixed constant.

$$mc^2 = I_{\text{topo}} \cdot \frac{\hbar c^3}{2\pi\ell}$$

The angular frequency is $\omega = 2\pi / T = 2\pi c / \ell$. The energy of a single quantum of information circulating in the knot is $\hbar \omega = 2\pi \hbar c / \ell$. Then:

$$mc^2 = I_{\text{topo}} \cdot \hbar \omega = I_{\text{topo}} \cdot \hbar \cdot \frac{2\pi c}{\ell}$$

Thus:

$$m = I_{\text{topo}} \cdot \frac{2\pi \hbar}{\ell c}$$

which matches our formula if we identify $I_{\text{topo}}/(2\pi)$ with the earlier factor. The **rest mass counts how many times light (or information) circulates around the knot per unit time**.

## What Time Interval?

The time interval is the **period of the internal time crystal** $T = \ell / c$. For a proton, $\ell \approx 1\,\text{fm}$, so $T \approx 3\times 10^{-24}\,\text{s}$. This is the natural clock of the knot. When we write $c = \ell / T$, then $c^2 = \ell^2 / T^2$. The equation $E = mc^2$ can be read as:

$$E = m \cdot \frac{\ell^2}{T^2}$$

But $m$ itself is proportional to $\hbar / (\ell c)$ times the number of cycles, so everything cancels consistently.
