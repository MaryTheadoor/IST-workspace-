# The Loom Beneath Space
## Information Substrate Theory for Curious Readers

*A conceptual introduction to an unfinished theory — what it claims, what it has actually computed, and how it could be proven wrong.*

---

### 1. The question nobody can answer

Physics is very good at *how* and strangely quiet about *why*. Why does the proton weigh 1836.15 times as much as the electron — not 1800, not 2000? Why is the strength of electromagnetism the particular number we call the fine-structure constant, about 1/137? Why does space have three dimensions rather than two or seven? Our best theories take these numbers as input. We measure them, we write them into the equations by hand, and the equations work magnificently. But nothing in those equations explains why the numbers are what they are. They are the arbitrary-looking dials of the universe, set by nobody-knows-what.

Information Substrate Theory — IST — is an attempt to answer "why" from below. Its premise can be stated in one sentence: **what we call particles, forces, and even space itself are patterns in a deeper, simpler object — and the dials of physics are set by the geometry of that object.** This paper explains that object, and the chain of reasoning that leads from it to specific, checkable numbers, using as little mathematics as possible. It also explains, honestly, where the theory currently succeeds and where it does not.

### 2. Three ideas you already believe

The construction builds on three ideas that are not controversial.

**First: nature is made of waves.** Quantum mechanics replaced little billiard-ball particles with wave-like excitations, and a century of experiment has confirmed it. An electron is not a tiny sphere; it is a persistent pattern in something that ripples.

**Second: waves need somewhere to happen.** A water wave needs water; a sound wave needs air. A quantum "wave" needs — what? Physics usually answers: spacetime itself. IST asks the next question: what if spacetime is *also* a ripple, in something simpler still? This is not a new kind of question. Einstein taught us that gravity is not a force pulling masses together but the shape of spacetime responding to them; matter and geometry turned out to be one system. IST pushes the same move one level down.

**Third: knots are real physics.** This one may be less familiar. In certain materials — liquid crystals, superfluids, magnetic films — physicists routinely create *knotted* disturbances: little loops of the material's internal field tied into shapes that cannot be undone without cutting. In 2019, researchers at the University of Colorado grew entire crystals made of knots. A knot is nature's way of storing information robustly: unlike a ripple, which fades, a knot stays tied. Keep this in mind; it becomes the heart of the theory.

### 3. The substrate: a sheet with a twist

IST proposes that beneath everything sits a two-dimensional object — a sheet, though "sheet" is already saying too much, because at this level there is not yet any space for the sheet to sit *in*. Better: the substrate is a two-dimensional pattern of relationships, a mathematical fabric of connections. What makes it special is one structural feature: **it has a twist.**

Picture an ordinary strip of paper. It has two sides. Now give the strip a half-twist and join the ends — you get a Möbius band, which famously has only *one* side: an ant crawling along it returns to its starting point flipped. The IST substrate is the two-dimensional cousin of that construction (mathematically, a Klein bottle: a surface with no distinction between inside and outside). The half-twist is not decoration. It is the theory's single structural ingredient — the one assumption from which everything else is supposed to follow.

What does a twist buy you? Two things, and both turn out to be load-bearing.

**Direction.** On an ordinary sheet, a pattern can wiggle but has no preferred handedness. The twist creates a seam — a place where crossing over flips you. Threads of information that run along the substrate come in two kinds: those that cross the seam an even number of times, and those that cross it oddly. This is the theory's candidate origin for the most basic duality in physics: positive and negative charge, matter and antimatter, the two directions of time's arrow. In IST, duality is not an extra ingredient; it is what the twist *is*.

**Half-integers.** A full turn brings you back to where you started; the twist means a *half* turn leaves you flipped, and it takes two full trips to come home. Physicists will recognize the deep signature of spin-½ particles — electrons and quarks, which in quantum mechanics must be "rotated twice" to return to themselves. In IST this stops being a mystery and becomes a tautology: matter is made of threads on a twisted sheet, and twisted sheets are where half-turns live.

### 4. Where do dimensions come from?

Here is the theory's most unusual idea, and it is worth slowing down for. We are used to thinking of dimensions — length, width, height — as the stage, given in advance. IST treats them as *achievements*.

Start with the substrate before any pattern exists on it: a state of perfect, contentless balance. The theory calls this the **zero point** — not "nothing," but undifferentiated potential, like a perfectly still pond. Now let the stillness waver: the smallest possible event is a single oscillation, a to-and-fro. That oscillation defines a *first* direction — not a direction in space (there is no space yet) but a distinction between "this way" and "that way" along the pattern.

A sustained oscillation needs a little more structure: it must carry its own momentum of becoming, and that requires a second direction. This, in the theory's language, is why the fundamental fabric is two-dimensional: a sheet is the minimal object that can bear a wave. Propagation needs one axis; amplitude needs another. Below two dimensions there can be rhythm but not waves; the sheet is where waves — and therefore physics — first become possible.

And the third dimension? Here the answer is delightfully mundane. One sheet is a world of ripples. But oscillations can synchronize: sheet-like patterns can lock onto one another, and the *relationship between layers* — the direction in which they stack — becomes a new dimension in its own right. Height, on this view, is not a pre-existing emptiness but the name we give to the coupling between layers of the fabric. Space grows by condensation, the way frost grows on a window: not all at once, but as structure crystallizes out of vibration.

This is not as exotic as it sounds. Several mainstream research programs in quantum gravity — with names like causal dynamical triangulations and asymptotic safety — have independently found hints that the dimensionality of space might not be fixed, but can *flow* with scale, running from four dimensions at large scales toward two at the smallest. IST is, in a sense, that same movie run in reverse: dimension crystallizing upward from nothing, rather than melting downward toward nothing. The movie-reversal is a genuinely different claim, but the underlying idea — dimension is dynamical, not God-given — now has respectable company.

### 5. Why three dimensions? The knot argument

If dimensions are achieved rather than given, why stop at three? The theory's answer is its most elegant piece of reasoning, and it uses the knots from Section 2.

Think of a shoelace. In our three-dimensional world you can tie it, and the knot stays tied. Now imagine creatures confined to a two-dimensional world, like drawings on paper: can they tie a knot? No — on paper, every "knot" is fake; any crossing can be undone by sliding the line around, because there is no *over* and *under*. Knots cannot exist in two dimensions. Now go the other way: in four dimensions, any knot can be untied. This is a theorem, not a guess — the extra dimension always gives the loop enough room to slip free of itself. Knots are impossible below three dimensions and impermanent above it. **Three dimensions is the unique dimensionality in which knots — persistent, information-storing structures — can exist at all.**

So if particles are knots in the fabric (and the theory claims they are — knotted threads of oscillation, stabilized by the twist), then the existence of persistent matter *selects* three dimensions. Not because three was decreed, but because it is the only setting where anything can last long enough to be called a thing. A fourth spatial dimension would not be forbidden; it would be self-erasing — a region where nothing knotted, and therefore nothing material, could survive.

The twist gets a final promotion here. Mathematicians have known for a century that a Klein bottle cannot live in three dimensions without intersecting itself — it fits perfectly in four, but forced into three it must pass through its own skin. IST reads this as physics: the seam, the twist, the charge duality — all of it is the price the substrate pays for existing in the only dimensionality where knots, and hence matter, can persist.

### 6. Particles, then, are knots — and light is different

The picture assembles quickly now. A massive particle — an electron, a quark — is a knot: a thread of the substrate tied through the seam, carrying twist, hence charge, hence mass (mass, in this language, is the energy cost of keeping a knot tied; E = mc² becomes the exchange rate between tied-ness and energy). The heavier the knot, the more thread it takes.

Light is the exception that proves the rule. A photon, in IST, is not a knot but a *coordinated vibration*: a double strand running on both faces of the fabric at once, linked through the seam like the rungs of a ladder — the theory's informal name for it is the "double helix" photon. Because it uses both faces symmetrically, it carries no net twist: no charge, no mass, and a perfect mirror symmetry that massive knots lack. This difference between knotted matter and balanced light is not decorative. It becomes the theory's flagship experiment, in Section 9.

### 7. The golden ratio: the harmonics of the loom

Anyone who has heard of IST has probably heard this part: the golden ratio, φ ≈ 1.618 — the number that governs sunflower spirals and nautilus shells — keeps appearing in its results. This is the part of the theory most likely to raise eyebrows, so it deserves a careful explanation, because there is a right way and a wrong way for φ to show up.

The wrong way would be numerology: hunting through lists of constants until something matches φ to a few decimal places, then declaring victory. Any large enough search will always find matches by coincidence — this is the well-known "look-elsewhere effect," and the theory's own internal audits (which we will come back to) are built specifically to guard against it.

The right way is the way φ appears elsewhere in nature. Phi shows up whenever a system grows by simple self-reference: each stage is the sum of the previous two. That rule generates the Fibonacci sequence — 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 — whose ratios converge on φ, and it is why φ governs the spiral packing of seeds and leaves: it is the signature of *recursive growth*. Crucially, it is also the signature of a certain kind of lattice. When a discrete structure cannot repeat periodically — when it can only tile itself in a never-repeating, self-similar pattern, like Penrose tilings — the ratio of structure at successive scales is forced toward φ. The golden ratio is not a mystical constant; it is the mathematics of self-similar growth in a discrete medium.

And the IST substrate is exactly such a medium: discrete, self-referential, growing by recursion. In the theory's computer model, φ is not inserted anywhere. It *emerges* — as the ratio the substrate's renormalization (its scale-change dynamics) converges to, matching the theoretical Fibonacci limit to nine decimal places. Where precisely does it show up in physics? The theory has a sharp, testable answer: **in the properties of bound, confined, emergent states — not in the bare couplings.** The masses of composite particles (the proton, the neutron, the meson octet) carry golden structure; the raw force strengths do not. That dividing line is itself a prediction, and it has held up across the theory's internal audits.

There is even an experimental precedent, from a completely different corner of physics. In 2010, a team led by Radu Coldea measured the excitations of a one-dimensional magnet made of cobalt niobate and found that its two lowest energy levels occur in the golden ratio — the fingerprint of an emergent symmetry (the exceptional Lie group E₈, for specialists) appearing in bound states, exactly where the dividing-line principle says such structure should live. One experiment does not validate a theory, but it demonstrates that nature really does produce φ in quantum bound-state spectra. IST's claim is that this is not a curiosity but a law of the loom.

### 8. What the theory has actually computed

Talk is cheap; the reason IST deserves a hearing is that it produces numbers. A few, stated plainly:

- **The proton-to-electron mass ratio**, measured as 1836.152, is matched by the theory's formula to within two parts in a hundred thousand — and the formula is not a fit with adjustable knobs; it is a closed combination of the structural ingredients (it reads "three times the volume of the symmetry space of the strong force," with the volume computed in substrate units).
- **The neutron–proton mass difference** — a small, delicate number on which the chemistry of the universe depends — comes out within a hair of the measured value, inside experimental uncertainty.
- **A curious empirical pattern in the charged-lepton masses** (the "Koide relation"), unexplained since 1981, falls out at the parts-per-million level.

The theory's record-keeping matters as much as its hits. Every relation it has ever tested — nearly seventy at last count — is logged in a registry with its outcome: supported, derived, failed, or demoted. Failures are published in the same breath as successes: a predicted golden pattern in heavy quark families failed by enormous margins, and the failure is in the registry; an apparent cosmological signal that initially looked strong was later traced, by the collaboration's own audit, to a modeling artifact, and loudly retracted in the project documents. This is what the theory's defenders point to when asked why IST is not numerology: it counts its misses, and it lets its own machinery kill its own darlings.

### 9. The experiment that could kill it

A theory earns the name by risking its life. IST's biggest wager concerns the quantum vacuum and light.

Quantum electrodynamics — the most precisely tested theory in history — predicts that empty space is not quite empty: light can scatter off light, with a strength set by virtual electrons flickering in and out of existence. The prediction comes with a specific internal ratio between two "polarization characters" of the effect (the parity-even and parity-odd pieces; think of them as the effect's response to left-handed versus right-handed arrangements of light). QED says that ratio is 7/4.

IST says it is **zero.** The reason goes back to Section 6: if the photon is a perfectly balanced two-faced structure, the vacuum it builds cannot prefer either handedness, so the parity-odd channel must vanish identically. No parameter can be adjusted to split the difference — the prediction is 0.000 against 1.750, a qualitative difference wearing quantitative clothes. Experiments that bounce intense light off intense light — the PVLAS collaboration's magnetic birefringence cavities in Italy, and planned photon-collider programs at X-ray free-electron lasers — are approaching the sensitivity where the two answers can be told apart. If the parity-odd channel shows up at the QED value, IST's photon is wrong, and with it a load-bearing wall of the edifice. That is exactly as it should be.

### 10. Black holes: where the theory gets speculative (and says so)

One extension is offered explicitly as a conjecture, and labeled as such. If knots require three dimensions and dissolve in a fourth, what happens where the fabric is crushed hardest — inside a black hole? The theory's suggestion: the interior is the one place where a *local* fourth dimension opens, knots dissolve, and structured matter reverts to unstructured information. The "singularity," on this reading, is not a point of infinite density but the visible face of the zero point — matter's patterns erased back to potential. Hawking radiation then reads as the slow re-release of that information, untied thread by untied thread. It is a compelling picture that happens to rhyme with where mainstream black-hole physics has been heading for decades (information is conserved; horizons store it; radiation returns it). But rhyme is not proof, and the collaboration treats this as a research direction, not a result.

### 11. What is not yet done

Honesty requires a list. The bare coupling constants — the actual strengths of the forces — remain un-derived; the theory's golden structure lives in bound states, and the deepest dial (1/137) is still a dial. A grand cosmological prediction (a gentle oscillation in the dark-energy density with a period set by the logarithm of φ) currently suffers a factor-of-thirty-eight mismatch between the theory's coupling and what the data would need — unresolved, and flagged as the top open problem. The emergence of gravity from the substrate is sketched, not closed. And the whole edifice rests on a computational model whose connection to a rigorous continuum field theory is still being built. The collaboration's working rule: the theory is a *research program with a scoreboard*, and the scoreboard, not the rhetoric, is the pitch.

### 12. Why bother?

Because the alternative is to accept that the deepest properties of matter are arbitrary — that the universe's dials were spun at random and we simply live with the readings. The history of physics counsels otherwise. The arbitrary "specific heats" of nineteenth-century chemistry became quantum mechanics; the arbitrary "atomic weights" became nuclear physics; the arbitrary "spectral lines" became quantum electrodynamics. Again and again, a table of unexplained numbers has turned out to be the shadow of a simpler structure underneath.

IST proposes that the structure underneath is a twisted sheet of pure relation, growing its own dimensions, tying itself into matter, vibrating as light, growing by the same recursive rule that spirals a sunflower. Perhaps it is wrong — the vacuum-birefringence experiments may well say so within a decade. But if it is even partly right, then the strange, specific numbers of particle physics are not arbitrary at all. They are the harmonics of the loom.
